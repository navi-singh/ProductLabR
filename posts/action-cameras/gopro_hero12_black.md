## Overview: a better HERO11, not a new imaging platform

The HERO12 Black improves the parts of an action camera you touch and configure more than the part that gathers light. Its built-in tripod thread, easier controls, HDR video, Bluetooth audio and improved stabilization make it a more capable tool than the HERO11 Black. But the **1/1.9-inch 8:7 sensor and 27MP resolution are familiar**, and the camera loses GPS. That is a thoughtful refinement, not a generational image-quality leap.

This is an **independent-source synthesis** of GoPro's published specifications and testing by TechRadar, PCMag and Tom's Guide. Verdict Hound did not perform hands-on testing. GoPro announced the camera on **September 6, 2023**, with store availability scheduled for **September 13**; its U.S. launch MSRP was **$399.99**. That is historical pricing, not a claim about today's availability or price.

In bright conditions, the HERO12 remains a persuasive little camera: sharp, smooth footage, lots of frame-rate options and an unusually flexible tall sensor. But two questions should come before the mode list: how much will you pay for a camera that shares its sensor with the HERO11, and do you shoot where heat or poor light matters more than resolution?

## Image quality: the tall sensor is the real advantage

The 8:7 sensor captures a near-square frame that can be reframed into **16:9**, **9:16** or other supported formats after recording. That is genuinely useful for a rider who wants one clip for a conventional video and a vertical social post. You can compose once and choose the crop later, rather than physically rotating the camera. The advantage is flexibility, not more detail than the sensor records.

GoPro lists video up to **5.3K at 60fps**, **4K at 120fps** and **2.7K at 240fps**, with available combinations varying by aspect ratio, color profile and stabilization. The high frame rates are useful for short slow-motion moments, while 5.3K gives editors room to crop for a 4K output. Neither mode guarantees better footage if the light is poor or the final video is watched on a phone.

Independent reviews broadly agree on the camera's daylight character. TechRadar found the HERO12's well-lit footage crisp and lively; PCMag saw more resolved detail in bright conditions than from DJI's 4K-only Osmo Action 4. Tom's Guide also reported good detail and dynamic range in its New York field recordings. Those are tested observations, not Verdict Hound measurements.

The limits are equally important. PCMag and TechRadar both found that the smaller GoPro sensor loses ground in dim scenes, where the Action 4's larger **1/1.3-inch** sensor can produce cleaner, brighter footage. A high resolution cannot recover shadow detail that the camera never captured. If your main footage is dusk rides, indoor events or night walks, the HERO12 is a poor choice at anything near its original price.

GoPro's HDR mode can retain more highlight and shadow detail in scenes with mixed lighting. PCMag's comparison footage showed a visible difference in a bright-sky scene, but HDR has mode limits: the highest frame rates are available in SDR, and HDR does not automatically pair with every resolution and aspect ratio. GP-Log is for a different buyer. It offers a flatter, **10-bit** file for color grading, but it looks subdued until edited and adds work to a simple share-and-go workflow.

## Stabilization: the HERO12's most dependable strength

HyperSmooth **6.0** with AutoBoost is the camera's clearest reason to choose it over an older or cheaper model. TechRadar tested it on running and a remote-control car; PCMag found footage steady when movement changed from running to walking. Their observations support a practical conclusion: for mounted daytime action, the GoPro can make rough motion easier to watch without a separate gimbal.

That stability is not free. Electronic stabilization crops the image, and AutoBoost varies that crop as motion changes. This is usually a good trade when the camera is bouncing around, but it can make the field of view feel less consistent. Keep the mount rigid and decide whether the wider view or the smoother frame matters more for a particular shot.

Horizon Lock is powerful but mode-dependent. GoPro's launch materials describe full **360-degree** horizon locking in Linear + Horizon Lock, or across lens settings with the optional Max Lens Mod 2.0. It is not a blanket promise that every resolution and lens setting will hold a level horizon through a full rotation. Check the mode table before relying on it for a specific shot.

For regular biking, skiing and handheld travel footage, stabilization scores higher than the camera's sensor age might suggest. For a fixed camera pointed at a stage or interview subject, HyperSmooth matters much less; the thermal behavior and recording duration become the more consequential tests.

## Battery and heat: runtime claims need their conditions

GoPro's launch announcement claimed up to **70 minutes** at 5.3K60, more than **95 minutes** at 5.3K30 and more than **155 minutes** at 1080p30, all with HyperSmooth 6.0 on. These are manufacturer figures, not independent outcomes, and they should not be read as a promise for every environment or setup.

The independent tests show why conditions matter. PCMag recorded about **30 minutes** at 5.3K60 and **40 minutes** at 4K60 in a roughly **70°F** room before thermal shutdown; the camera's top reached about **115°F**. TechRadar, by contrast, recorded for over **30 minutes** at 5.3K in outdoor use around **70°F / 20°C**, and reported more than an hour of continuous 4K60 on an Enduro battery. The tests differ in settings and conditions; those outcomes should not be averaged into one expected runtime.

The buying takeaway is less dramatic than either a marketing headline or a single thermal cutoff. The HERO12 is a good fit for shorter clips while moving in ordinary outdoor conditions. It is a riskier choice for a long, stationary recording at a demanding setting, especially in warmth or with limited airflow. If a lecture, performance or interview cannot be repeated, test the exact settings in the intended environment and bring external power or another camera.

The included removable Enduro battery is **1720mAh**. Carrying a spare is sensible for a full day, and USB-C power can extend a stationary setup, but an attached cable can complicate a waterproof or rugged configuration. GoPro also removed built-in GPS from the HERO12. If location overlays are part of your routine, the HERO11 may be the better purchase despite being older.

## Design and controls: the tripod socket is a practical upgrade

The body weighs about **154g** and retains GoPro's built-in folding mounting fingers. The new **1/4-20** thread lets it attach to a standard tripod without an adapter. For travel creators switching between a helmet mount, a handheld grip and a small camera tripod, that is a quality-of-life improvement rather than a cosmetic detail.

The front **1.4-inch** display helps with framing, while the rear **2.27-inch** touchscreen is the main control surface. PCMag found the revised Easy Controls clearer for casual use, with Pro Controls available when a user wants to set frame rate, aspect ratio, color and stabilization directly. Easy mode is helpful for getting started, but advanced users should expect to spend time learning which settings work together.

The camera is manufacturer-rated waterproof to **10m (33ft)** without a housing. That rating assumes the doors and seals are properly closed and intact; it is not an independent pressure test. A dedicated protective housing is rated for deeper use. Check the specific accessory and the camera's sealing instructions before taking it underwater.

GoPro's extensive mount and accessory ecosystem is an advantage if you already own compatible gear. It is less persuasive as a reason to buy the camera from scratch: mounts, replacement batteries, the optional Max Lens Mod and audio accessories add to the total cost. The HERO12's standard thread reduces one adapter purchase, but it does not make every accessory included in the box.

## Audio and editing: flexibility with a quality caveat

Bluetooth audio is a meaningful addition for narration when the camera is mounted out of reach. The HERO12 can connect to supported Bluetooth audio devices, including earbuds and microphones, and preserve a separate audio track. That convenience is not the same as a professional wireless microphone system. TechRadar's tests with different earbuds showed that the result depended heavily on the specific device; a dedicated mic performed better.

The built-in microphone is fine for close speech in calm surroundings, but wind and vehicle noise are hard problems for any small action camera. The optional Media Mod adds a directional mic and a **3.5mm** input for external equipment. Consider it a separate purchase and a less sealed rig, not a free improvement to the bare waterproof camera.

The Quik app makes remote control and quick social edits easier. A GoPro subscription adds cloud and editing features, but it is not a prerequisite for recording or for using desktop editing software. If you do not want another subscription, confirm that your chosen transfer and editing workflow works without the paid features before buying around the software pitch.

## HERO12 vs. HERO11, DJI Action 4 and Insta360 ONE RS

The **GoPro HERO11 Black** is the closest alternative. It shares the core 8:7, 27MP imaging platform and many capture modes. The HERO12 adds HDR, Bluetooth audio, a 1/4-20 thread and a new control experience, but removes GPS. If the HERO11 costs notably less and you do not need those additions, it is the smarter buy. If GPS is essential, favor the HERO11 or verify a different model's location features before switching.

The **DJI Osmo Action 4** is preferable for dimmer scenes and deeper water. PCMag's comparison notes the larger sensor and cleaner low-light image; its published waterproof rating is **18m** bare. The trade is less maximum resolution and no equivalent 5.3K, 8:7 GoPro workflow. Choose it when light sensitivity matters more than aggressive cropping or high-resolution slow motion.

The **GoPro HERO13 Black** is the more relevant GoPro alternative if the two are close in price. It keeps the 8:7, 27MP sensor and familiar 5.3K60 ceiling, but restores GPS, adds a larger **1900mAh** battery, magnetic mounting and HB-Series lens options. TechRadar's testing found improvements in heat dissipation and runtime, though not a new sensor or resolution. Choose HERO13 if you need those specific changes; if you do not, the HERO12's discount can still make sense.

At the HERO12's **$399.99 September 2023 launch MSRP**, those tradeoffs were easier to justify. In 2026, do not treat that historical number as a current deal. Compare real offers for the body, batteries and accessories you need; if HERO13 is close in price, the older camera's missing GPS and smaller battery become harder to overlook.

## Who should buy it—and who should pass

Buy the HERO12 Black if you shoot mostly in daylight, value reliable electronic stabilization, want one tall recording to supply both vertical and widescreen edits, and can get it at a meaningful discount. It also makes sense for existing GoPro owners whose mounts already fit and who will use HDR, GP-Log, Bluetooth audio or the tripod thread.

Skip it if GPS overlays are part of your footage, if most of your work happens after dark, or if you need long unattended recording at high resolutions. A cheaper HERO11 can do much of the same imaging work; the DJI Action 4 is a stronger low-light and bare-water alternative; the modular ONE RS offers a different creative capability. A buyer should not pay the old flagship price merely because the camera can list impressive frame rates.

## Verdict: buy for stabilization and reframing, not the model number

The HERO12 Black is a capable action camera whose real upgrades are usability, mounting and workflow. Its stabilized daylight footage is excellent, and the 8:7 sensor remains an unusually practical format for creators publishing in more than one shape. But image quality in low light has not caught up with larger-sensor rivals, stationary high-load recording needs a realistic thermal plan, and GPS is gone.

Our six equally weighted ratings average **8.1/10**, or **4.0/5**. Image Quality earns 8.5, Stabilization 9.0, Battery & Thermal Performance 7.0, Build & Mounting 8.5, Audio & Workflow 8.0, and Value 7.5. The value score assumes a genuinely discounted offer, not the historical MSRP. Recommendation: buy it used or on sale for bright, moving footage and flexible reframing; otherwise compare the HERO11 and DJI's larger-sensor options before paying a premium for an older flagship.

## FAQ: practical buying questions

### When was the HERO12 Black released?

GoPro announced it on **September 6, 2023** and said it would be available in stores worldwide on **September 13, 2023**. The U.S. launch MSRP was **$399.99**; this review does not claim that is a current price.

### Is the HERO12 Black a major image-quality upgrade over the HERO11?

No. The two share a 1/1.9-inch, 8:7, 27MP sensor. The HERO12 adds HDR video and other workflow refinements, but its strongest reasons to upgrade are those features, the tripod thread and Bluetooth audio—not a new sensor.

### Is its 5.3K60 runtime really 70 minutes?

That is GoPro's launch figure, not a guarantee. Independent tests reported different results depending on ambient conditions and whether the camera was stationary or in outdoor use. Rehearse a long recording at your own settings.

### Does it have built-in GPS?

No. GoPro removed GPS from the HERO12. If you need location data or overlays, check that an alternative camera supports your required workflow before buying.

### Can I use one recording for vertical and landscape video?

Yes. The 8:7 capture gives you room to crop into supported formats such as 16:9 and 9:16. The exact resolution and frame-rate combinations depend on the selected mode.

### Is the HERO12 waterproof without a case?

GoPro rates the sealed camera to 10m (33ft). That is a manufacturer rating, not a guarantee for damaged seals, improperly closed doors or every type of underwater impact. Follow the manual and use a housing when the conditions call for one.

### Do I need a GoPro subscription to use the camera?

No for recording. A subscription is tied to optional services such as cloud backup and certain editing benefits; you can transfer and edit footage through other supported workflows.

### Is Bluetooth audio good enough for vlogging?

It is convenient for basic narration, particularly when the camera is mounted away from you. Audio quality depends on the paired device, and independent testing found dedicated microphones clearer. Test your own microphone in wind and motion before relying on it.

### Should I buy the HERO12 or the DJI Osmo Action 4?

Choose the HERO12 for 5.3K capture, 8:7 reframing and GoPro's stabilization and accessory ecosystem. Choose the Action 4 if low-light performance and a deeper bare-camera water rating matter more than maximum resolution.

### Is HERO13 worth paying more for?

Consider it if you want GPS, magnetic mounting, HB-Series lenses or its larger battery and improved thermal performance. Both cameras retain the same basic sensor and 5.3K60 limit, so a modestly priced HERO12 remains sensible when you do not need those additions.

### Is the HERO12 a good camera for long interviews or stage recordings?

It can record stationary footage, but independent testing observed heat-related stops during demanding indoor recording. For an important long take, test the exact resolution, frame rate, power arrangement and room conditions in advance; do not rely on the manufacturer's best-case runtime alone.
