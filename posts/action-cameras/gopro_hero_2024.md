## Overview: small enough to wear, not simple enough to forget

The GoPro HERO (2024) makes one appealing promise: take a tiny, screen-equipped action camera, put it on a helmet or a bag, and capture uncomplicated clips without carrying a flagship. At **86g**, it does that part well. The catch is that this is not a miniature HERO13 Black. It trades away the flagship’s flexibility, and the absence of in-camera stabilization changes what happens after you press record.

GoPro announced the HERO on **September 4, 2024** at a U.S. launch price of **$199.99**; its announcement said retail availability would begin on **September 22**. Those are historical launch details, not a quote for today. We have not verified a current price. GoPro describes the camera as **35% lower in volume and 46% lighter than HERO13 Black**, while the company’s product page documents its **4K30** video, **2.7K60** slow motion and **12MP** stills. [GoPro’s announcement](https://investor.gopro.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2024/GoPro-Announces-Two-New-Cameras-The-399-HERO13-Black-and-the-199-HERO/default.aspx) and [product page](https://gopro.com/en/us/shop/cameras/learn/hero/CHDHF-131-master.html) are the primary references for those specifications.

This is an independent-source synthesis, not a Verdict Hound hands-on test. It draws on Sam Kieldsen’s tested [TechRadar review](https://www.techradar.com/cameras/action-cameras/gopro-hero-review-the-action-camera-king-goes-back-to-basics) and Adam Juniper’s cycling-focused [Digital Camera World review](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/reviews/gopro-hero-4k-review). Their shared conclusion is useful: the camera’s size and straightforward controls have real appeal, but its pared-back feature set asks buyers to accept compromises that a compact body alone cannot justify.

The decisive question is not whether this GoPro is small. It is whether you are happy to stabilize footage in Quik, live with a battery you cannot change, and accept 4K30 as the ceiling. If yes, the weight makes sense. If not, the savings over a more capable camera are likely to show up as editing friction rather than a better-value setup.

## Design and controls: a proper screen in a very small package

The HERO avoids a common tiny-camera annoyance: it still has a rear **1.76-inch LCD touchscreen**. Both reviewers found the camera easy to handle and operate, and the screen gives you a basic way to frame, select one of the capture modes and check a clip. Its controls are deliberately sparse: the interface is less intimidating than the many menus on a premium action camera, but that simplicity also means little room to tune the image to a particular activity.

At **86g**, the compact body is the product’s strongest physical argument. Juniper specifically liked it on a cycling helmet because the weight did not pull the mount out of position during his use. That is a relevant benefit: reduced mass can matter more on a helmet or a small adhesive mount than it does in a hand-held travel shot. It does not, by itself, make footage steadier; mount placement and the camera’s electronic stabilization still determine the result.

GoPro keeps its familiar folding mounting fingers on the bottom, so existing GoPro-style mounts can be reused. You do not need a proprietary cage just to attach the camera to a common mount. The manufacturer rates the bare HERO for **5m** of water resistance and specifies a replaceable, water-repelling lens cover. Treat the depth rating as GoPro’s claim, not an independent pressure test; close the doors carefully and follow the manual before taking it underwater.

The design’s neatness is also where the first ownership compromise appears. The Enduro battery is built in and cannot be swapped. A removable pack lets a rider carry a spare and continue recording; this camera instead needs charging time or an external power arrangement. The permanent battery helps GoPro keep the body small, but it makes a long day less forgiving if you cannot recharge between sessions. It also makes battery condition a more consequential part of long-term ownership.

There is a second practical omission: no external microphone input. The integrated microphones are enough for ambient sound and casual clips in calm conditions, but they are not a path to a separate wind-protected mic or a deliberate speech setup. A helmet-mounted camera can be perfectly adequate for a visual record and still be the wrong tool for narration.

## Image quality: adequate daylight, little room to rescue a shot

GoPro and the independent reviews describe a **1/2.8-inch sensor**. The official page lists **4K video**, while GoPro’s launch documentation specifies **4K30** and **2.7K60** slow motion. These modes are useful for basic daytime action and social clips, but they are a narrower set than the more adjustable capture options on higher-tier cameras. There is no 4K60 mode to fall back on when you want smoother motion at 4K resolution.

Kieldsen found the image quality disappointing beside pricier action cameras, describing the video as flat and soft, with low-light footage losing detail and gaining noise. Juniper’s assessment was less severe but still restrained: he found the camera “fine” in decent light, with some detail retained in evening samples but detail loss where subjects moved. Those are independent reviewers’ observations, not our own test results. Together, they support a sensible limit: expect usable daytime records, not a camera that makes poor light look good.

The HERO’s files are **8-bit** with a maximum bitrate of **50Mbps**, according to TechRadar’s specification and image-quality coverage. The number alone does not determine how good a clip looks, but it helps explain why this is not the right camera for heavy color grading or demanding detail retention. There is no log profile to provide extra grading latitude. If you want footage ready to share with minimal editing, that may be acceptable; if you expect to match it to another camera, the lack of image controls becomes a real constraint.

The camera records in **4:3** using its **165-degree lens**, GoPro says, with the Quik app able to create a wider 16:9 SuperView crop or a vertical crop. That gives you some flexibility after filming, but it is not the same as having high-resolution vertical and horizontal modes ready in-camera. The practical trade is straightforward: record a broad view and choose a crop later, or accept less flexibility and compose specifically for the intended frame.

GoPro lists **12MP** still photos and **8MP** frame grabs from video. Those figures are sufficient for casual sharing, but they should not be mistaken for a substitute for a dedicated photo camera. The tiny sensor, fixed capture modes and limited image controls all point toward the same use: short clips in good light, where light weight matters more than fine detail or post-production latitude.

## Stabilization and workflow: the real cost is after recording

The most important distinction from HERO13 Black is not the number in the product name. HERO13 applies HyperSmooth in-camera; on this HERO, GoPro says HyperSmooth is applied in the Quik app. GoPro’s product FAQ is explicit: footage viewed on the camera display has no stabilization applied, and clips need to be stabilized and exported from Quik before using them in another editor.

That workflow is manageable if you usually transfer clips to a phone and share a few highlights. It is a nuisance if you want to move a card into a desktop editing workflow and work immediately. It also creates a second version of the footage and an extra processing step. The camera can capture a bumpy ride, but the file on the card is not yet the stabilized result many buyers assume the GoPro name guarantees.

Kieldsen reported that Quik stabilized exported clips effectively, but also encountered crashes while exporting large files to an iPhone. Juniper likewise describes app steps as part of the trade-off. Neither observation proves every owner will have the same software experience; both underline that the app is not an optional polish step for stabilization. If your editing habits already involve Quik, the extra step may be acceptable. If your desktop editor is the center of your workflow, it is a poor fit.

The camera’s three headline modes—video, photo and slow motion—are a welcome menu for a beginner, not a broad creative toolkit. Independent reviews note missing time-lapse and other familiar action-camera options. You also cannot change the image as extensively as on a flagship. This is the meaningful difference between “simple” and “easy”: the camera is easy to operate, but the lack of in-camera stabilization and configuration can make the complete process less simple than the sparse menu suggests.

## Battery and endurance: strong for a sealed camera, still a single charge

GoPro advertises up to **100 minutes** of continuous recording at its highest video setting. That is a manufacturer figure, not a result we replicated. In his TechRadar review, Kieldsen reported **80 minutes of continuous 4K30 recording** from a fully charged camera sample and more than **2.5 hours** when recording **1080p30** in battery-saver mode. Those are reviewer test results under his conditions, and not a promise that every temperature, card or setting will produce the same runtime.

That 4K30 result is respectable for a very small action camera. It supports Juniper’s view that the HERO can suit cycling and long-running daytime capture better than some smaller alternatives. The limitation is not only the duration; it is the inability to swap in a fresh battery. On a trip with several separate filming windows, a camera with a shorter runtime but replaceable packs may be more convenient than one that records longer and then needs to charge.

The sources we reviewed do not establish a repeatable overheating problem for this specific HERO, so we do not score it down for a heat failure. Nor should the Enduro label be read as proof of unlimited recording. Long, fixed recordings depend on the scene, settings and airflow; test the actual setup before relying on it for an event. The more certain ownership constraint is simply that the battery is built in.

## Alternatives: do not compare it to HERO13 by name alone

The **HERO13 Black** is the clearest comparison because GoPro launched both cameras together. The flagship adds **5.3K60** recording, swappable Enduro batteries and in-camera HyperSmooth, according to GoPro’s announcement. The HERO is much lighter and costs less at its historical launch MSRP, but it is not a HERO13 with the front display removed. Choose the HERO13 if you need more control, a higher resolution ceiling or the ability to keep shooting by changing batteries; choose the small HERO only when its reduced weight is central to the job.

The **Insta360 GO 3S** makes more sense when the smallest possible camera matters more than a standard action-camera body. Kieldsen describes it as roughly half the HERO’s weight and size while still recording 4K. That is a different kind of convenience: compare how each camera attaches and how its workflow fits your activity, rather than assuming “small” means the same thing across both designs. His TechRadar HERO review mentions it as a smaller alternative, while Juniper compares its battery appeal with the Go 3S.

The **DJI Action 2** is another compact camera to consider if you want a small-form-factor alternative and are willing to look beyond GoPro’s mount ecosystem. TechRadar’s review names it as a competitor, but we would not treat its 2024 quoted price as a current comparison. Check current availability and the exact configuration before buying; the Action 2 is an older product, and stock or bundle contents can vary. The useful comparison is whether a modular compact camera better suits your mounting needs—not whether a launch-era price makes it appear equivalent.

If you are choosing between those cameras, start with the actual recording flow. The HERO’s screen and folding fingers are compelling for a lightweight setup; its app-processed stabilization and non-removable battery are not. A more expensive body can be the cheaper choice if it saves time on every transfer or lets you carry spare power.

## Who should buy it—and who should skip it

The HERO (2024) is a reasonable fit for someone who wants a simple daytime camera on a helmet or bag, already uses GoPro mounts, and is comfortable stabilizing clips in Quik. It is also approachable for a family member who would rather point and record than navigate advanced controls. Its light weight is not a vague lifestyle benefit: it can reduce the burden of a helmet-mounted setup, exactly the use Juniper found appealing.

Skip it as your primary camera if you need stable files directly from the card, clean footage in dim light, 4K60, external audio or an interchangeable battery. Those needs are not edge cases for creators; they define a large share of action-camera use. A fixed battery is especially hard to work around once it is depleted, and no amount of app simplicity fixes that during an activity.

It is also not a good bargain just because it has the GoPro name and a small body. At the historical **$199.99** U.S. launch price, its compromises were already substantial. As of this review, we have not verified a live price, so we cannot make a current value claim. Compare the price of a complete kit—including a compatible microSD card and any mounts you actually need—against a discounted higher-tier model before deciding.

## Verdict: buy for low weight, not for the GoPro badge

Our six category scores average **6.3/10** (equivalent to **3.2/5** on the site’s star scale). The HERO earns its best marks for build and mounting (**8.5/10**) and battery performance (**8/10**), which recognize the low mass, familiar mounting fingers and independently reported 4K30 runtime. Image quality and stabilization each score **5.5/10**: daytime clips can be serviceable, but the independent tests identify weak low-light performance and stabilization that requires a phone or computer workflow. Audio and workflow rate **4.5/10**, and value is **6/10** because current pricing has not been verified and the concessions are unusually consequential.

Buy the GoPro HERO (2024) when a featherweight, screen-equipped body is the deciding feature and you accept processing your clips in Quik. Do not buy it expecting a smaller HERO13 Black, a one-step desktop workflow or dependable all-day shooting without a recharge. Its best reason to exist is clear; so is the price of that small size.

## FAQ: practical questions before you buy

### Is this the same camera as the HERO13 Black?

No. The HERO (2024) is the tiny entry-level model announced alongside HERO13 Black, not the flagship with a smaller body. It records up to 4K30 and processes stabilization in Quik; HERO13 Black has higher-resolution recording and in-camera HyperSmooth.

### Does it record 4K at 60 frames per second?

No. GoPro specifies 4K30 video and 2.7K60 slow motion. If 4K60 is a requirement, choose a different camera.

### Is stabilization active while recording?

No. GoPro says HyperSmooth is applied in Quik after recording. You need to process and export footage from the app before using it as stabilized video elsewhere.

### Can I replace the battery?

No. The Enduro battery is built into the camera. You can recharge it, but you cannot swap in a spare pack.

### How long does it record on one charge?

GoPro’s launch claim is up to 100 minutes at its highest video setting. TechRadar’s independent test reported 80 minutes of continuous 4K30 recording from a full charge; treat that as one reviewer’s result, not a universal guarantee.

### Is it waterproof without a housing?

GoPro rates the bare camera to 5m. Follow the manufacturer’s sealing instructions and use the appropriate accessories for conditions beyond that rating.

### Can I attach an external microphone?

The independent reviews report no external microphone input. The built-in microphones are the available onboard audio option, so buyers who need a lavalier or cleaner speech recording should choose another setup.

### Does it need a subscription to stabilize video?

GoPro documents stabilization through the Quik app, but the sources reviewed do not establish that a subscription is required for the stabilization/export step itself. Subscription benefits are separate; check GoPro’s current plan terms before paying for cloud storage or other services.

### Is the HERO a better buy than an Insta360 GO 3S?

It depends on the mounting and editing workflow. TechRadar describes the GO 3S as smaller and lighter while also recording 4K; the HERO offers a conventional rear screen and built-in GoPro mounting fingers. Compare current bundles and the way each camera attaches to your gear.

### Should I buy one at the original $199.99 price?

That was the U.S. launch MSRP, not a verified current offer. We would only consider it if the light weight and simple capture are more valuable to you than in-camera stabilization, battery swapping, external audio and stronger low-light performance.
