## Overview: the first DJI 360 camera now needs an ownership discount

The original Osmo 360 is appealing for reasons that do not depend on winning a resolution race. Its 105GB of usable internal storage reduces forgotten-card anxiety, and its direct support for compatible DJI microphones gives creators a more useful dialogue option than trusting wind processing alone. Those benefits remain real after the sequel arrives.

The catch is the non-replaceable outer lenses. On a camera that looks both ways, glass damage is a central ownership risk rather than an obscure failure case. Osmo 360 II changes that, so the older model now needs a meaningful total-price advantage and appropriate local support to be our preferred purchase.

[TechRadar's Sam Kieldsen](https://www.techradar.com/cameras/360-cameras/dji-osmo-360-review) dates the launch to July 31, 2025, listing £409.99 for the UK Standard Combo and limited initial US availability. We do not convert that into a current US retail offer. Verify the seller, warranty region and exact kit before treating an import price as a straightforward bargain.

Verdict Hound has not tested the Osmo 360. This assessment uses [DJI's specifications](https://www.dji.com/360/specs), [its support FAQ](https://www.dji.com/360/faq), independently reported results and our buying analysis. The scores belong to our six-metric rubric, not to another reviewer's rating.

## Design and build: compact, but with two vulnerable faces

DJI lists a 183g body measuring 61 × 36.3 × 81mm. The 2-inch OLED display has a specified peak brightness of 800 nits. Its shorter, wider shape differs from the taller X-series layout; which feels easier to hold is a preference, not a performance result established by dimensions alone.

The square 1/1.1-inch sensors are marketed as giving 1-inch-equivalent panoramic coverage. That is not a claim that DJI installed the physical 1-inch sensor type in the ONE RS 1-Inch 360 Edition. The square format may use the lens image efficiently, but the sensor wording should not substitute for actual image evidence.

Its quick-release arrangement fits the intended Osmo mounting system and works alongside a tripod attachment. Confirm exact compatibility rather than assuming every third-party action-camera accessory fits safely. Keeping a projecting camera secure matters more than how quickly it attaches.

The lack of user-replaceable outer lenses is the main hardware reservation. A cap protects the camera in a bag; careful placement protects it between takes. Neither creates a repair system. Budget and support decisions should include what happens after a scratch, especially if you expect the camera to travel on an exposed pole.

The body has an IP68 rating and a manufacturer 10m waterproof limit. DJI separately discourages camera-only underwater capture because refraction can cause distortion and poor stitching. That optical warning is central: “waterproof” does not mean every underwater video is useful.

## Image quality and performance: use the official mode list

DJI specifies spherical 8K at up to 50fps, 6K at up to 60fps and 4K at up to 100fps. The 8K frame dimensions are 7680 × 3840. Single-lens capture offers 5K/60fps, with a 4K/120fps Boost option. Keep the single-lens modes separate from the full-sphere modes.

TechRadar's prose includes an inconsistent spherical 5K/60fps reference; the official specification gives 6K/60fps, which we use here. The manufacturer also identifies 120MP spherical stills, not “120MB” as an isolated third-party typo suggests. Source checking is more reliable than repeating a compact summary table unquestioningly.

Kieldsen found high detail in good light and effective results from the larger square-sensor system. His comparisons did not establish a clean victory over X5 in every scene. He also saw oversharpening and strongly rendered blue skies in some output. That supports a strong image score without pretending the default processing is neutral or universally superior.

Whole-sphere 8K is not an equally detailed flat crop in every direction. A narrow view uses part of the source, and an aggressive zoom exposes limits. The Osmo 360 is most useful for choosing a moderately wide viewpoint later, not for finding telephoto-quality faces on the distant horizon.

The SuperNight mode is specified for 8K/30fps and 6K/30fps spherical work. It offers a difficult-light processing choice, not every maximum frame rate at once. Decide whether cleaner low-light material or higher temporal resolution matters more for the actual shot.

## Color and stabilization: useful tools, not universal fixes

Ten-bit D-Log M gives creators a grading path with more room for color adjustment than a simple ready-to-share profile. That requires a managed edit and appropriate conversion. A log option is not a guarantee that untreated footage will look attractive on an ordinary display.

RockSteady and HorizonSteady support smoother-looking output, while spherical capture gives substantial reframing freedom. TechRadar found the stabilization effective in its moving footage. A level horizon still does not remove every mount vibration or exposure blur.

DJI's official minimum stitching distance is 0.75m, and the focus specification is 0.35m. These describe different boundaries. Something can be within optical focus range yet still be a difficult close stitching subject. Keep faces and important objects away from the join and give the camera enough surrounding space.

Before an important shoot, inspect a sample in the final crop. Look at near foreground joins, movement and color across the lenses. That is a practical recommendation, not a claim of a Verdict Hound test. If you only inspect the forward preview, you may miss an obstruction on the other side.

## Editing and setup: activation is part of the purchase

DJI says initial activation requires the Mimo app and a compatible smartphone. That is a real setup dependency, not a detail to discover after arriving somewhere without the appropriate device or connectivity. Check the current operating-system requirements and legitimate app-distribution instructions before buying.

For edits, Mimo supports a mobile route while DJI Studio offers desktop reframing. Panoramic recordings use DJI's OSV format; conventional single-lens files use MP4 with HEVC support. A spherical source is not automatically a standard flat file any application understands.

The independent review found the core editing path approachable, with tracking and framing tools useful for completed clips. A desktop workflow is especially worth considering when you need several exports or deliberate color work. Check the current version's features rather than treating every marketing integration as delivered.

As with other 360 systems, retain the original if you want another perspective later. A single flat export cannot restore directions you discarded. Internal storage helps collect footage, but a separate archive and an organized offload process preserve the camera's main creative value.

Do not buy the camera to avoid deciding what the audience should watch. A 360 source allows more choices; it does not make a story automatically. Select meaningful keyframes and keep perspective changes readable. That effort is part of the product category, not a defect unique to DJI.

## Audio: the ecosystem advantage is specific, not unlimited

TechRadar reports wind interference from the built-in microphones as a continuing problem. That is a stronger reason to consider direct microphone pairing than a generic assertion that processed onboard sound is enough for every activity.

The same reviewer used a Mic Mini while cycling and obtained clearer voice capture. DJI's FAQ confirms direct support for Mic 2 and Mic Mini. We keep the recommendation to those verified models rather than assuming every transmitter with a DJI name shares the same connection behavior.

A worn microphone changes where sound is captured, so it can address a problem software on the camera cannot fully solve. Placement and wind protection still matter. Test levels and the intended connection before relying on narration during a moving activity.

For a person who already owns compatible audio hardware, the ecosystem can reduce extra accessories. For someone starting from nothing, include the microphone in the total budget. An ecosystem advantage is not free value if you have to buy all of it first.

## Battery, internal storage and heat planning

DJI's 1950mAh battery claim is 100 minutes at 8K/30fps in 25°C laboratory conditions with the screen off and specific wireless, voice and assistant functions disabled. That detail matters. It is not a promise of the same duration at 8K/50fps or with every convenience feature active.

Kieldsen recorded about 90 minutes at 8K/30fps on a full battery. This corroborates a useful order of endurance for that workload without meeting the exact manufacturer figure. We attribute it as a reported test result, not a Verdict Hound measurement or a universal minimum.

Separate manufacturer numbers refer to Endurance behavior and a lower-demand 6K/24fps workload. They should not be compared directly with another camera's high-resolution normal-mode test. Matched mode labels are more helpful than a chart of the largest available runtime numbers.

The 128GB nominal internal capacity provides 105GB usable, with microSD expansion officially supported up to 1TB. A card and planned offloads are still sensible for longer trips. The built-in space is a useful safety net, not proof that you can capture and retain unlimited high-resolution material.

Battery and storage breaks can be combined. Check both before the next session, then verify lens cleanliness and the selected mode. The most impressive runtime figure is irrelevant if the next clip records through a fingerprint or into the wrong format.

## Alternatives: sequel, X5 and a premium Insta360 route

The [Osmo 360 II](/articles/dji_osmo_360_ii) directly addresses replaceable lenses and adds native 8K/60fps. Choose it at a similar supported total price unless the older model meets a distinct budget requirement. The sequel's reported high-frame-rate indoor heat limit deserves attention rather than assuming newer means unlimited recording.

The [X5](/articles/insta360_x5) combines better-supported difficult-light capability with replaceable optics and an extensive local editing route. Its app has its own independently reported friction. Compare the devices and audio equipment you already own rather than declaring one ecosystem effortless.

The [X6](/articles/insta360_x6) is a premium option with newer imaging, internal storage and strong reported 8K/30fps battery performance. Its beta-mobile-app caveat can make desktop fit especially important. A higher launch price does not by itself establish a better purchase for a casual daylight creator.

The [X4](/articles/insta360_x4) is an older daylight value option when discounted, but it also lacks user-replaceable underlying lenses and has weaker difficult-light evidence. Compare actual local pricing and warranty instead of using currency conversion to invent a neat global ranking.

Our moderate value and durability scores reflect that ownership context. Imaging, stabilization and recording perform strongly enough to recommend the original for a suitable discounted supported kit; lens repair and regional uncertainty prevent a blanket recommendation.

## Who should buy it, and who should skip it

Buy the original Osmo 360 for a meaningful supported discount, especially if compatible DJI microphones and a checked editing device are already part of your kit. Its internal storage and reported 8K/30fps endurance are tangible conveniences.

Skip it if easy outer-lens replacement is essential or if the sequel costs only a little more with the same warranty and accessories. Also skip any listing that leaves activation, local service or the exact kit unclear. A low import price is not automatically a low-risk purchase.

## Verdict

DJI's first 360 camera has a defensible identity: strong spherical capture, useful internal memory and practical audio connections. The sequel exposes the older model's most consequential weakness, which is what happens when its glass is damaged.

Choose it on total value and support, not on the highest headline alone. Its best current buyer is someone who can use the ecosystem benefits and obtain a real discount without trading away service clarity.

## FAQ

### Does Osmo 360 have physical 1-inch sensors?

The official sensors are dual 1/1.1-inch square CMOS, described in 1-inch-equivalent panoramic terms. That differs from a physical 1-inch sensor designation.

### Is its faster spherical mode 5K/60fps?

The official mode is 6K/60fps. Single-lens 5K/60fps is separate. We use the official specification rather than an inconsistent third-party shorthand.

### Can its lenses be replaced like Osmo 360 II's?

Not through the sequel's user-replaceable outer-lens system. Check service options for underlying damage and protect the glass during storage and use.

### Can I activate it without a smartphone?

DJI's FAQ requires Mimo on a compatible smartphone for initial activation. Verify current device requirements before purchasing or traveling with an unactivated camera.

### Does internal storage eliminate cards?

It provides 105GB usable and can save a forgotten-card session. Whether that is enough depends on your footage volume and modes. Expansion and offloads remain useful.

### Which DJI microphones are verified here?

Mic 2 and Mic Mini direct support is confirmed by the original camera's FAQ. Do not assume compatibility with every later microphone from the same brand.

### Is the 100-minute figure measured by Verdict Hound?

No. It is a manufacturer claim under stated laboratory conditions. TechRadar reports about 90 minutes at 8K/30fps in its own battery test.

### Does 10m waterproofing mean good underwater stitching?

No. DJI explicitly discourages camera-only underwater capture because refraction can create distortion and stitching problems. Read the current accessory guidance for your intended underwater setup.
