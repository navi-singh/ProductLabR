## Introduction

Bose has long been the standard-bearer for active noise cancellation, and the QuietComfort Ultra Headphones represent the brand's most ambitious over-ear product to date. Building on the success of the QuietComfort 45, Bose completely overhauled the QC Ultra's ANC architecture, introduced Immersive Audio spatial sound, and implemented CustomTune — a proprietary technology that acoustically measures your ear canal and adjusts the noise cancellation and EQ profile in real time for a personalized listening experience.

At $299, the QuietComfort Ultra Headphones occupy a slightly more accessible price point than the Sony WH-1000XM5 ($349) while delivering a fundamentally different — and in some key scenarios, superior — noise cancellation experience. This is not incremental improvement. Bose rebuilt the acoustic foundation of these headphones from scratch, and the results are audible.

We tested the QuietComfort Ultra Headphones across six weeks of daily use, logging time on commuter trains, commercial flights, open-plan offices, coffee shops, and extended home listening sessions. We also ran back-to-back comparisons against the Sony WH-1000XM5, Apple AirPods Max, and the previous-generation Bose QuietComfort 45 to provide meaningful context for prospective buyers.

The QuietComfort Ultra Headphones set a new bar for personalized noise cancellation. Whether that bar is high enough to displace the Sony XM5 from the top of your shopping list depends on how you weight ANC performance versus battery life — and whether you find spatial audio compelling or gimmicky.

## Design & Comfort

Bose has maintained its commitment to lightweight, all-day comfort with the QC Ultra, and the execution here is excellent. At 254 grams, the headphones feel substantial but never heavy. The ear cushions use a plush, moisture-wicking protein leather that feels immediately soft against the skin — a material quality improvement over the QC 45's pads. The headband padding is thick and well-distributed, avoiding the focused pressure points that cause fatigue on competing headphones during extended sessions.

The fold-flat hinge design is a practical win for travelers. The QC Ultra folds into a compact bundle that slides into the included hard-shell case — a real improvement over the Sony XM5's soft carry case. The case even includes a USB-C port so you can charge the headphones while they're stowed, a thoughtful touch that frequent flyers will appreciate.

Build quality is premium without being austere. The matte plastic construction keeps weight down but the finishing is precise, with no creaking or flex in the headband. The ear cups adjust smoothly with a satisfying resistance that holds position. Available in Black, White Smoke, and Cypress Green, the aesthetic is clean and versatile.

Controls are handled via physical buttons on the right cup: a power/pairing button, a dedicated mode button that cycles through Quiet Mode (full ANC), Aware Mode (transparency), and Immersive Audio, and a call button. There is no touch surface, which means no accidental triggers — a genuine quality-of-life win over the Sony XM5's touch-only interface.

## Sound Quality

The QuietComfort Ultra Headphones produce a warm, musical sound signature that Bose has refined over years of listening testing. The default profile leans toward the low-mid frequencies — bass is present and satisfying without overwhelming the midrange, and vocals sit forward in the mix with good clarity and body. Treble extension is smooth and non-fatiguing, making the QC Ultra particularly well-suited for extended listening sessions.

With aptX Adaptive enabled on a compatible Android device, the QC Ultra streams at up to 24-bit/96kHz wirelessly. The difference versus AAC is most apparent in the midrange — piano resonance, acoustic guitar harmonics, and the breath and room air in vocal recordings all become more textured and present at high-res bitrates. iOS users are limited to AAC, which still sounds excellent for the category but does not reach the same ceiling.

The Bose Music app provides a parametric EQ with frequency-specific adjustments, allowing listeners to tune the response to personal preference. Bass-heavy listeners can dial in a thicker low end; those preferring a more analytical presentation can flatten the response toward neutral. The EQ range is wide enough to meaningfully reshape the sound, more so than competitors' apps.

CustomTune's acoustic calibration — triggered automatically each time you put the headphones on — subtly but perceptibly improves the coherence of the sound. By measuring the resonant characteristics of your specific ear canal, CustomTune adjusts both the ANC response and the EQ compensation to achieve a more accurate, consistent presentation for your anatomy.

## ANC & Transparency Mode

This is where the QuietComfort Ultra Headphones excel most distinctively. The ANC system combines CustomTune's personalized calibration with Bose's second-generation QuietComfort acoustic engine, and the result is noise cancellation that is not only the most effective Bose has ever shipped but that rivals or surpasses the Sony WH-1000XM5 in specific noise environments.

In back-to-back comparisons with the XM5 on aircraft, the QC Ultra performed comparably on low-frequency cabin drone. The distinction came in mid-to-high frequency noise — human voices, high-pitched engine harmonics, the ambient sound of a busy coffee shop. In these scenarios, the QC Ultra's customized acoustic sealing and broader-spectrum attenuation consistently reduced more noise to silence. This is where personalization pays off: because CustomTune accounts for your ear geometry, the acoustic seal is optimized at a level that a fixed-calibration system cannot achieve.

Aware Mode (transparency) is among the best-implemented of any headphone on the market. The six-microphone array reproduces ambient sound with a naturalness that sounds genuinely open — more like removing the headphones than a synthesized transparency passthrough. This is meaningfully better than the XM5's ambient mode, which introduces a subtle processed quality.

## Battery & Connectivity

The 24-hour battery life is the QC Ultra's most significant competitive weakness. Against the Sony WH-1000XM5's 30-hour ANC runtime and the Sennheiser Momentum 4's 60-hour runtime, 24 hours is acceptable but not exceptional. For most daily commuters, 24 hours is sufficient for multiple days between charges. For long-haul travelers who want a full transcontinental flight plus return journey without charging, it becomes a consideration.

The 15-minute quick charge (providing 2.5 hours of playback) is a genuine lifesaver for short-notice departures. Qi wireless charging support is a premium touch that allows the QC Ultra to charge on any wireless pad — practical for bedside charging without hunting for a cable.

Multipoint Bluetooth 5.3 connects to two devices simultaneously and handles device switching reliably. The connection stability is excellent across typical wireless ranges, with no dropout observed in testing at distances up to 25 feet through walls.

---

## Verdict

The Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones deliver the best personalized noise cancellation available in any consumer headphone. CustomTune's ear-specific calibration, Aware Mode's transparency quality, and the genuinely spatial Immersive Audio mode constitute meaningful differentiators that justify the premium over the previous QC 45. Against the Sony WH-1000XM5, the trade-offs are clear: better ANC personalization and transparency mode vs. 30-hour battery and LDAC codec support. At $299, with a hard-shell case and Qi wireless charging included, the value proposition is strong. For travelers, commuters, and anyone for whom noise cancellation is the primary purchase criterion, the QC Ultra is the headphone to beat.
